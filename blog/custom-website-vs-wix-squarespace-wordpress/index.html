Custom website vs Wix, Squarespace or WordPress: which is right for your business?

Website builders are genuinely good at what they do. The question is whether what they do is what your business needs. Here’s a fair comparison.

By Pabloch Tech · 27 September 2026 · 2 min read

On this page

  1. When a website builder is the right call
  2. When WordPress makes sense
  3. What “hand-built” actually means
  4. Side by side
  5. The honest recommendation

Key takeaways

  • Website builders (Wix, Squarespace) are best when you want to build and edit everything yourself and don’t mind renting the platform.
  • WordPress is flexible but depends on themes and plugins that need regular updates.
  • A hand-built custom website costs more up front than a builder, but you own it outright, it loads fast, and it has no plugins to break.
  • If your site needs a custom feature — an estimator, a booking flow, a quote form — custom usually wins.

When a website builder is the right call

Wix and Squarespace make it possible to get online in an afternoon with no code. If you enjoy designing, want to change things daily yourself, and your needs are simple, a builder is a sensible choice. You pay a monthly fee, and the platform handles hosting and security.

The trade-offs: you rent rather than own, your site can only do what the platform supports, and moving away later usually means rebuilding from scratch. Templates also mean your site can look a lot like your competitors’.

When WordPress makes sense

WordPress powers a large share of the web and can do almost anything with the right theme and plugins. That flexibility is also its weak point for small businesses: each plugin is written by a different developer, needs updating, and can conflict with the others. Unmaintained WordPress sites are a common target for attacks, so someone has to keep on top of updates.

What “hand-built” actually means

A hand-built website is written directly in HTML, CSS and JavaScript for your business, with a back end added only when it’s needed — for example a store’s checkout. There’s no builder platform underneath and no plugin stack. In practice that means:

  • Speed. Pages ship only the code they need, so they load quickly — especially on phones and slow connections, which matters to visitors and to search engines.
  • Ownership. You own the code and can host it anywhere. No subscription keeps it alive.
  • Fewer moving parts. Nothing updates itself overnight and breaks the layout.
  • Exactly what you need. Features like an instant cleaning-price estimator or a photo-upload quote form are built around how your business works.

Side by side

  • Upfront cost: builder lowest · WordPress varies · custom a one-off fee (Pabloch Tech from $400).
  • Ongoing cost: builder monthly subscription · WordPress hosting plus maintenance · custom hosting plus optional care.
  • Ownership: builder rented · WordPress owned · custom owned.
  • Custom features: builder limited to its apps · WordPress via plugins · custom built to fit.
  • Maintenance: builder handled by the platform · WordPress frequent updates · custom minimal.
  • Who edits it: builder you · WordPress you or a developer · custom your developer, or you for simple changes.

The honest recommendation

If you want to do it all yourself and your needs are simple, start with a builder. If you want a site that’s fast, looks like no one else’s, does something specific for your customers, and belongs to you — go custom. The easiest way to decide is to see what custom would look like for your business before paying for it.

Common questions

Is a custom website better than Wix or Squarespace?

It depends on your needs. Builders suit people who want to edit everything themselves and are happy renting the platform. A custom website suits businesses that want speed, full ownership and features built around how they work.

Do custom websites rank better on Google?

No platform guarantees rankings, but hand-built sites are typically fast and lean, with clean markup and structured data, which gives search engines a solid technical foundation.
